The lifecycle

From raw candles to a frozen forward test

1 · Data engineering

Expired and live option candles, spot, futures and India VIX from Upstox, aligned so every price is observed at the same instant. Resumable collection with manifests.

2 · Pricing and features

Black-Scholes implied volatility and Greeks, a leave-one-out volatility surface, price deviations and put-call parity residuals. All features causal by construction.

3 · Prediction

Will a pricing gap shrink by half within 30 minutes? XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost and stacked ensembles, validated in time order with purging.

4 · Deep-learning hedging

MLP and GRU policies trained with a CVaR objective on simulated GBM and Heston paths, benchmarked against delta and band hedging under costs.

5 · Paper trading

Bid/ask fills, fees and margin in a restart-safe ledger. 20 accounts run in parallel on identical positions. The code cannot place orders.

6 · Validation

A hash-chained protocol written before the test starts, an independent ledger audit and predeclared pass or fail criteria.

Deep learning for risk management

Can a learned hedge beat delta hedging?

Tail risk falls in simulation, simple baselines still compete

MLP and GRU policies trained on CVaR

Instead of optimising average return, the policies minimise the average loss in the worst 5% of outcomes (CVaR95). Training paths come from Geometric Brownian Motion and the Heston stochastic-volatility model; evaluation adds transaction costs, position limits and whole-lot rounding.

The neural policy lowers loss CVaR95 against delta hedging in every world tested, but the reduction shrinks when the volatility model is harder.

GBM paths−12.8%
Bootstrapped paths−9.1%
Heston paths−6.2%

Reduction in loss CVaR95 versus delta hedging; scale 0 to 20%. Best single result: 0.00993 against 0.01181 (−15.9%) at a cost of 0.001 per unit traded.

What simpler methods did better

Benchmark against the boring alternative

A tuned no-trade band delivered the better mean P&L. A GRU was 1.8% worse than a feed-forward network (memory had nothing to add), ensembles added nothing, and whole-lot rounding kept the trained policy about 17% above the continuous optimum while a rounded conventional hedge came within about 1%.

Lesson the project is built around: always benchmark the sophisticated model against the simplest credible alternative, under the same costs, before believing it.

What it found on real data

Real signal, no edge after costs

Signal is real and transfers

Predicting whether a pricing gap closes

The classifier separates gaps that close from those that do not on later data, and the frozen model then held on an earlier year it had never seen (227 sessions, no refitting). Pushing the score far higher was tested as a stress test and ruled out: about a third of labels sit within half an index point of the cut-off, where tick noise decides them.

Costs decide

Why a good classifier still does not pay

The price move after entry is statistically zero, while each round trip costs about ₹430–450 in spread, slippage, brokerage, charges and tax. A gap closing is a statement about distance to a model price, not about the option's own P&L.

Hedging buys safety, not profit: it cuts the worst-5% loss by 59% for about ₹2,500 of mean P&L per position.

Engineering discipline

Why the numbers can be trusted

  • Found and fixed its own data bug. Option and spot candles were paired by start time instead of close time. Correcting it changed 515,903 of 519,725 spot values (median 7.4 index points) and flipped 8.45% of comparable labels. Everything downstream was rebuilt.
  • Leakage controls that are tested. Training labels must finish before a validation block starts, with a one-session gap. Feature builders are tested to give identical values when all later data is removed, and shuffled labels score near chance.
  • Live features equal batch features. A bar-by-bar feature service reproduces the stored datasets exactly and the frozen model's probabilities to about 1e-16 in replay, at roughly 1.5 seconds per bar.
  • Evidence that cannot be edited quietly. The forward-test criteria, code hashes and allowed models are frozen in a hash-chained file. Lowering the 60-day bar is rejected on load. Changing one simulated fill by half a rupee is caught by an independent ledger replay as a ₹130 discrepancy.
  • Negative results reported. A recurrent policy, ensembles, a classifier-assisted hedger, profit-based targets and a far higher score target were tested and did not win. Each is documented with its interval.
  • Operational safety. Malformed input is quarantined, a single writer holds each account, closes retry on failure, CI runs on Python 3.12 and 3.13 with lint and type checks, and the test runner blocks sockets so no test can reach the vendor.

What happens next

A forward test with the rules fixed in advance

Four option contracts (at-the-money and one percent out-of-the-money, calls and puts) across five hedging strategies make 20 paper accounts. Fills use recorded bid and ask with fees and margin. The first three sessions are shakedown days; the test then needs 60 trading days.

Dry run on a stored Friday session (real candles, synthetic quotes): 840 events, every account reconciled, nothing left open. It verifies the software and says nothing about markets.

Limits

What this does not show

  • No profitable trading edge. Hedged results lose to costs in every experiment.
  • Fills use configured fee and margin proxies on candle data. Real order-book depth and partial fills are untested until the forward test runs.
  • Later-period results were reviewed while building the models, so they are retrospective. The earlier-year check tests regime transfer. Only the forward test can confirm anything going forward.
  • The real-time feature service is verified in replay but not yet connected to the paper-trading loop, so machine-learning entries have not run live.
